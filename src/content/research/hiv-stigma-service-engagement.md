## Overview

This work focuses on understanding how HIV-related stigma shapes engagement across prevention, testing, and treatment within complex health systems.

I use participatory systems methods to translate stakeholder knowledge into causal loop diagrams (CLDs), capturing how stigma operates across individual, healthcare, and community levels. These diagrams reveal feedback loops linking factors such as social support, care experiences, and service quality, and show how stigma can both reinforce and disrupt engagement in care.

This work is part of a broader effort to bridge participatory system mapping with data-informed modeling for decision support in public health systems.

## Key Insights

### 1. Stigma operates through reinforcing feedback loops
Anticipated and internalized stigma reduce social support, which lowers resilience and further decreases care engagement—creating self-reinforcing cycles.

### 2. Care experiences shape long-term engagement
Positive care interactions strengthen health literacy and self-advocacy, while negative experiences disrupt engagement pathways.

### 3. Community context determines direction of change
Community norms and visibility can either reinforce stigma or help reduce it, depending on local social dynamics.

## Implications

These system structures highlight leverage points for intervention, including:
- Improving quality of care experiences  
- Strengthening social support and community visibility  
- Enhancing provider training and accountability
