## Overview

Across my projects, I develop simulation models that can be used for scenario exploration, intervention evaluation, and policy decision-making. A central goal of this work beyond the modeling is to make our model outputs accessible beyond technical audiences.

We design decision-support dashboards that allow stakeholders to compare interventions, explore uncertainty, and understand how system changes may affect downstream outcomes. These tools are intended to translate complex model results into structured, interactive resources for researchers, implementation partners, and public health decision-makers.

## StigmaScope: HIV Stigma and Service Engagement

StigmaScope is a dashboard for visualizing HIV-related stigma and exploring how stigma mitigation could influence HIV prevention and care outcomes (forthcoming!). The goal of the dashboard is to bring stigma to the forefront of HIV planning by making stigma-related data, model outputs, and intervention scenarios more visible and accessible within HIV-related public health planning.

We work with a professional design and development team to translate our research content—including model outputs, conceptual explanations, intervention scenarios, and stakeholder-facing text—into a public-facing web resource. The dashboard is designed to serve public health researchers and county health departments by showing how stigma varies across geographies, how stigma relates to HIV service engagement, and how epidemiological outcomes may change if stigma is prioritized as part of HIV prevention strategy.

The dashboard also functions as a dissemination and tracking tool. It provides a structured way to communicate why stigma matters for HIV service engagement, compare geographic patterns, and explore potential intervention effects across the Ending the HIV Epidemic pillars.

## Service Delivery Redesign: Maternal Health in Kenya

The Service Delivery Redesign dashboard translates a hybrid agent-based and dynamical systems model of maternal health service delivery into an interactive scenario exploration tool. Initially developed for Kakamega County, Kenya, the model is currently also being adapted for Kisii, Mombasa, and Makueni Counties in Kenya.

A key contribution of the model is that implementation is represented as a quantitative driver of health outcomes, rather than as a background assumption. Users can explore how changes in demand generation, referral capacity, facility readiness, quality of care, staffing, equipment, and trust shape the effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of maternal health service delivery redesign.

The model is designed for adaptability across LMIC health system contexts. It preserves common system components, including primary care, higher-level referral facilities, emergency referral pathways, and the maternal care continuum, while allowing local data and assumptions to define how those components function in each county.

We designed and built the dashboard in Streamlit as an end-to-end decision-support tool, with scenario comparison features, maternal care pathway visualizations, system process indicators, health outcome projections, and cost-effectiveness outputs.
